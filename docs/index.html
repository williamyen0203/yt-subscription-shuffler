Subscription Shuffler

Never doomscroll your feed again.

Subscription Shuffler is a Chrome extension that plays a random video from your YouTube subscriptions — one click, straight into the video.

Get the extension

Purpose

Subscription Shuffler exists to help you rediscover the channels you already follow. Its sole purpose is to pick a random video from your YouTube subscription list and open it, so you can enjoy older uploads you may have missed instead of only ever seeing the newest ones in your feed.

  • It plays a randomly selected video from a randomly selected channel in your subscription list.
  • It reduces the manual effort of deciding what to watch.
  • It works entirely inside Chrome with your own Google account.

Features

Truly random

Picks a random subscription, then a random video from that channel's uploads — not just their latest upload.

Your data stays yours

Signs in with your own Google account. Subscriptions and exclusions live only on your device.

Exclude channels

Don't want a channel in the rotation? Exclude it with one click and see the full list anytime.

Lightweight

A simple popup that opens the video in a new tab. No background servers, no tracking.

How it works

After you sign in, the extension reads your subscription list with read-only access, shuffles it, picks a random video from the chosen channel, and opens it for you. Every shuffle is a fair roll — you might land anywhere in a channel's uploads.

What data does Subscription Shuffler use, and why?

To choose a random video, the extension needs to know which channels you subscribe to, so it asks you to sign in with your Google account. It requests read-only access to your YouTube data — just enough to:

  • read your list of subscriptions,
  • read the video list of the channel that was picked,
  • open the chosen video for you.

It cannot modify your subscriptions, upload anything, or access any other part of your Google account. All requests go directly from your browser to YouTube, and your data is stored only on your own device. The developer does not see, store, or receive any of your data.
